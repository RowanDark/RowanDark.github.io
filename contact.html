What to include in a first email

the faster path to a useful reply

A first email doesn't need to be formal, and it doesn't need every answer below — but the more of this it covers, the more useful the first reply can be, and the fewer back-and-forth emails it takes to get to a scoped quote:

Scope
What you want tested — application, network, external attack surface, or a combination
Size
Roughly how large the environment is — hosts, applications, or endpoints in scope
Driver
Whether there's a compliance deadline behind the engagement, and which framework, if any
Timeline
When you need testing done and a report delivered

You'll hear back from me directly — there's no support queue or account manager between your email and a reply.

How an engagement starts

three steps before any testing begins

Every engagement moves through the same three steps before any testing begins:

  1. 01

    Scoping call

    A conversation to understand what you need tested and what a realistic engagement looks like — no pitch deck, no pressure to sign anything on the call. If Rosec isn't the right fit for what you need, you'll hear that too.

  2. 02

    Written SOW

    A statement of work with rules of engagement and a signed authorization letter, covering targets, testing windows, exclusions, and the report delivery date.

  3. 03

    Testing begins

    Nothing is tested against your systems until all of the above is signed — that's a hard line, not a formality, regardless of how small or informal the engagement is.

None of this requires a call to get started — an email with what you'd like tested is enough to get a scoped statement of work back. The scoping call exists for when it's faster to talk through the environment than to write it all out.
