Services

penetration testing and security assessment, scoped to fit

Every engagement follows the Rosec methodology: scoped and authorized in writing, tested by hand, and delivered with a report a non-technical stakeholder can act on. Baseline scope is web application and network testing; wireless, physical, social engineering, and cloud configuration review are available as add-on modules. See Methodology for how an engagement runs end to end.

Web Application Penetration Testing

Manual, tool-assisted testing of a web application against the OWASP Web Security Testing Guide (WSTG) v4.2. Both unauthenticated and authenticated testing are performed, so findings behind a login are covered, not just the public surface. Emphasis is on business logic flaws and access control failures — the class of bug automated scanners consistently miss. You get a findings report scoped to the application's roles and workflows, with reproduction steps for every confirmed issue.

Typical engagement: 5–10 business days, depending on application size. Needed from you: scoped URLs or a staging environment, plus a test account for each user role. Deliverable: findings report and executive summary.

Network Penetration Testing

Testing of external and internal network perimeters, aligned to NIST SP 800-115. Coverage includes service enumeration, exposed configuration issues, and patch-level exposure across in-scope hosts. External testing assesses what's reachable from the internet; internal testing assesses what's reachable from inside your network, including lateral movement and segmentation gaps. You get a findings report mapped to the hosts and services tested.

Typical engagement: 5–10 business days for a single network segment. Needed from you: the in-scope IP ranges or host list, and a limited-privilege account for credentialed testing. Deliverable: findings report mapped to hosts and services.

External Attack Surface Assessment

Passive discovery of your internet-facing footprint — certificate transparency logs, DNS enumeration, and related open-source sources — to surface shadow IT and forgotten subdomains before someone else finds them. This assessment is non-intrusive: it does not touch discovered assets beyond passive lookups, requires no testing window, and causes no downtime. You get an inventory of discovered assets with an assessment of what's exposed and why it matters.

Typical engagement: 2–3 business days, non-intrusive. Needed from you: your primary domain name — this assessment doesn't touch your systems, so nothing else is required. Deliverable: asset inventory with an exposure assessment.

Remediation Retest

Validation that fixes from a prior engagement actually closed the reported findings, rather than just changed their symptoms. Each finding from the original report is retested individually and delivered against a retest tracker showing per-finding pass/fail status, so you have a clear record of what's fixed and what isn't.

Typical engagement: 1–3 business days, scoped to the findings being retested. Needed from you: confirmation that fixes are deployed to the environment originally tested. Deliverable: a per-finding retest tracker.

What we don't do

naming the limits up front

Being clear about what's out of scope saves both of us a wasted call:

  • No compliance audit sign-off — testing supports a SOC 2, PCI, or HIPAA effort, but Rosec doesn't issue the compliance attestation itself
  • No managed detection and response — engagements are point-in-time assessments, not ongoing monitoring of your environment
  • No incident response retainer — if you're currently dealing with an active breach, that's a different kind of engagement than what's offered here

FAQ

the questions that come up on scoping calls

Will testing take our systems down?

Testing is non-destructive by default. Anything that could crash a service, corrupt data, or cause an outage requires explicit written authorization before it's attempted, separate from the general scope agreement — see Methodology for the full safety policy.

Do you need production access?

It depends on what you want answered. A staging environment that mirrors production is often preferred for testing that could touch data, but production testing is common and safe within the non-destructive default above — this gets settled during scoping, not assumed either way.

How long until we get the report?

The delivery date is fixed in the statement of work before testing starts, not left open-ended — so you know the date going in rather than waiting to find out.

What happens if you find something critical mid-test?

Critical findings are never held for the final report. You're notified as soon as one is confirmed, with enough detail to start remediating immediately. If continuing to test that specific issue risks damaging data or availability, testing on it pauses until we've talked.
