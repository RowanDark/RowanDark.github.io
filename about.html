I'm the principal security consultant and owner of Rosec Cyber LLC, a penetration testing and security assessment practice based in Covington, Georgia. When you hire a small firm, you're hiring a person, not a logo — this page is about the person actually doing the testing, not a composite bio written by a marketing team.

Before moving into offensive security, I spent 15+ years in IT operations, systems management, and logistics and operations leadership. That's not a footnote — it's most of what separates this practice from a pure-technical competitor. Most testers have written exploits against systems they've never had to run. I've been the person paged at 2 a.m. because a change window went wrong, the person who had to explain an outage to operations leadership, and the person who had to keep a warehouse or a help desk functioning while IT changed underneath it. That's what a finding actually threatens, and it's why I score and write findings the way I do: in terms of what breaks for the business, not just what a scanner flags.

I've also been the person on the other end of a vague finding with no reproduction steps, so every report I deliver is written to be understood and actioned by the team that has to fix it, not just the person who reads it once. Alongside Rosec, I currently work as an IT project lead and serve as a Microsoft Dynamics 365 security subject-matter expert — ongoing, hands-on exposure to how a real enterprise environment gets configured, permissioned, patched, and occasionally broken, rather than something I only see from the outside during an engagement.

How I work

what shapes the way an engagement runs

These aren't marketing language — they're the specific habits that come out of having been on the receiving end of a bad vendor relationship, and having had to explain a security finding to people whose job isn't security.

  • Scoping honesty. If your environment doesn't need a two-week engagement, that's what I'll say on the scoping call — not scale the plan to fit a template.
  • Severity discipline. A finding is scored on what it actually does to your environment, not on how alarming it looks in a scanner's default output. A low stays a low.
  • Reporting for the room, not just the ticket. Every report carries an executive summary written for whoever has to explain risk to an owner or a board, alongside the reproduction steps the engineering team needs.
  • Direct line. You reach me directly — no account manager and no ticket queue between you and the person who did the testing.

Gray Box

threat intel for small business audiences

Gray Box is a cybersecurity podcast and Substack I write and record, aimed at translating threat intelligence into something a small business owner can actually use — without the jargon or the assumption that you have a security team. Episodes cover the same territory this site does: what a real attacker actually goes after in a small business environment, what a finding on a report means in plain terms, and what's worth fixing first when the budget doesn't stretch to everything. New episodes and writeups are published on the Gray Box Substack.
